# GTM Strategy

## Overview
Build a go-to-market strategy that ties channel selection, motion design, and budget to a specific stage and ICP. This item merges the Product Compass GTM framework (strategy + metrics + 90-day roadmap) with the workflows.io GTM stack (5 layers, channel matrix, motion selection, anti-patterns). Read both halves. The first half is for planning the launch. The second half is for fixing what is broken once you have data.

## When to Use
- Planning a product launch or entering a new market
- Auditing an existing GTM stack for gaps, redundancy, or misalignment
- Choosing between outbound, inbound, product-led, or partner motion
- Allocating budget across GTM channels and tools
- Diagnosing why pipeline is not converting despite high activity
- Creating a 90-day execution roadmap with KPIs

## Before You Start
Ask the user for, or confirm you have:
1. Product or service description, ACV range, sales cycle length
2. Stage (pre-revenue, $0-500K ARR, $500K-2M, $2M-10M, $10M+)
3. Current GTM motion (outbound, inbound, product-led, partner, none, or mixed)
4. Current stack (CRM, email, enrichment, analytics, content tools)
5. Monthly GTM budget (tools + ads + headcount)
6. What is working and what is not (best channel, worst channel, biggest bottleneck)
7. GTM team size (founder only, 1-2 people, dedicated team)
8. 90-day success target (pipeline $, meetings/month, revenue)

Do not recommend a stack or motion without understanding the user's current state. A pre-revenue founder needs a different playbook than a $5M ARR company with 3 SDRs.

## Part 1 — Strategy and Planning (Product Compass lens)

### Step 1: Gather Research Data
- Product description and key features
- Target market segment details
- Market research, validation data, or PMF survey results
- Competitive landscape (top 3-5 competitors, how they position, where you win or lose)
- Customer interviews, win/loss notes, NPS responses

### Step 2: Define Marketing Channels
Evaluate which channels best reach the target audience. Match to ACV, team capacity, and timeline:

- Digital paid: paid search, social ads, display, retargeting
- Content and inbound: blog, SEO, thought leadership, podcast, YouTube
- Sales outbound: cold email, cold calling, LinkedIn outreach, ABM
- Community and grassroots: Slack groups, Discord, Reddit, conferences, meetups
- Product-led and viral: free trial, freemium, referral, in-product prompts
- Partner and ecosystem: co-marketing, integrations, resellers, agencies

### Step 3: Develop Messaging
- Core value proposition stated in the buyer's language (not your marketing language)
- Key differentiators and competitive advantages
- Pain point mapping (which buyer pain → which feature → which proof point)
- Proof points and social proof (logos, named case studies, before/after numbers)
- Channel-specific variations (LinkedIn is not the same as cold email is not the same as the homepage)

### Step 4: Define Success Metrics
Set measurable KPIs across the funnel:

| Stage | Awareness | Engagement | Conversion | Revenue | Retention |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Metric** | Impressions, reach, branded search | CTR, time on site, reply rate | Signups, demos, trials | MRR, new ARR, ACV | NRR, churn, expansion |
| **Target window** | Weekly | Weekly | Weekly | Monthly | Monthly |

Always pair each metric with a baseline (where you are now) and a 90-day target (where you want to be). Without a baseline, the number is meaningless.

### Step 5: Create a 90-Day Launch Plan
Build a phased plan, not a single launch moment.

**Pre-launch (weeks 1-4)**
- Messaging finalized and tested with 5-10 customer conversations
- Channels set up (CRM, sequences, ad accounts, content production)
- ICP and target account list built
- 3-5 case studies or proof points ready
- Internal alignment: sales knows the pitch, support knows the FAQ, founders know the narrative

**Launch (weeks 5-8)**
- Channel activation (start small, measure before scaling)
- Outbound sequences go live with daily review of reply rates
- Content cadence established (3 posts/week LinkedIn, 1 blog/week, 1 podcast appearance/month)
- Paid channels running with daily budget caps
- Daily 15-minute standup for the first 2 weeks to fix what breaks

**Post-launch (weeks 9-12)**
- Weekly metrics review with the team
- Double down on what is working, kill what is not
- Iterate messaging based on real conversations
- Build the playbook so a new hire can run this in 30 days

**90-day decision point:** Either keep going, change the motion, or stop and reposition. All three are valid outcomes.

## Part 2 — Stack, Motions, and Budget (workflows.io lens)

### The 5-Layer GTM Stack
A modern B2B GTM stack has five layers. If any layer is broken, everything above it underperforms. Fix in order, do not skip.

**Layer 1: Data** — CRM is single source of truth, contact bounce rate under 5%, dedup monthly, ICP documented. Tools: HubSpot, Salesforce, Pipedrive, Close, Attio. Enrichment: Apollo, Clay, ZoomInfo, Cognism, Lusha.

**Layer 2: Enrichment** — Signal detection (job postings, funding, leadership changes, tech stack, website visits), account scoring, research automation. Tools: Clay (signals), Bombora, G2 Buyer Intent, RB2B, Common Room.

**Layer 3: Sequencing** — Multi-channel orchestration: email, LinkedIn, phone, ads. Sequences persona-specific, not one-size-fits-all. Tools: Instantly, Smartlead, Outreach, Salesloft, Lemlist (email); HeyReach, Expandi, Dripify, PhantomBuster (LinkedIn); Orum, Nooks, Aircall, Kixie (phone).

**Layer 4: CRM and Pipeline** — Deal pipeline with clear stage entry/exit criteria, automatic activity logging, weekly forecasting within 20% of actuals. Native to the CRM or tools like Dreamdata, Factors.ai.

**Layer 5: Analytics** — Attribution (first-touch, last-touch, or multi-touch), campaign performance by segment, CAC, LTV, payback, pipeline velocity, NRR. Tools: HubSpot Reporting, Dreamdata, Factors.ai, Databox, Looker Studio.

### Channel Selection Matrix
Match channel to ACV, team size, and timeline:

| Channel | Best For | Typical CAC | Time to Results | Team Needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cold email | SMB to mid-market, high volume | $50-500/meeting | 2-4 weeks | 1 person |
| LinkedIn outbound | Mid-market to enterprise, relationship | $100-800/meeting | 4-8 weeks | 1 person |
| Cold calling | Enterprise, high ACV, complex | $200-1,000/meeting | Immediate | Dedicated SDR |
| Content and inbound | All sizes, long-term brand | $100-400/meeting at scale | 3-6 months to ramp | Writer + distribution |
| Paid ads (LinkedIn) | Enterprise ABM, awareness | $300-2,000/meeting | 2-4 weeks | Budget + designer |
| Paid ads (Google) | High-intent search, bottom funnel | $100-800/meeting | 1-2 weeks | Budget + landing pages |
| Events and webinars | Enterprise, relationship | $500-2,000/meeting | 4-8 weeks per event | 2-3 people + speakers |
| Partnerships | All sizes, multiplier | Variable | 2-6 months to activate | Partner manager |

### GTM Motions
Four canonical motions. Most B2B companies should start outbound-led, then layer others as they grow.

**Outbound-led:** ICP → account sourcing → signal enrichment → multi-channel sequences → meeting → close. Best for clear ICP, $5K+ ACV, known problem. Fastest to revenue, depends on data and copy quality.

**Inbound-led:** Content → SEO/LinkedIn/YouTube distribution → website visit → lead capture → nurture → close. Best for strong content engine or natural search demand. Slow to start, compounds over time.

**Product-led:** Free trial or freemium → activation → usage milestones → in-app prompts + email nurture → self-serve upgrade or sales-assist. Best for SaaS with low ACV and high volume. Low CAC at scale, requires product investment.

**Partner-led:** Partner identification → co-marketing/co-selling → joint content + events → warm intros → close. Best for ecosystem plays or trust-driven sales. Slow, hard to control, never your only motion.

### The 70/20/10 Budget Rule
- **70%** on your primary motion (the one most likely to work given your ICP and stage)
- **20%** on your secondary motion (the one you are building toward)
- **10%** on experiments (new channels, new tools, new approaches)

Review this split quarterly. When an experiment in the 10% bucket produces results, promote it to 20% and shift something else down.

### Budget by Stage
- **Pre-revenue:** $500-2,000/mo. ~70% tools, ~20% LinkedIn Premium or content, ~10% test one paid channel. Zero on headcount.
- **$0-500K ARR:** $2,000-5,000/mo. ~50% tools, ~30% first GTM hire, ~20% content and experiments.
- **$500K-2M ARR:** $5,000-15,000/mo. ~40% headcount, ~30% full stack, ~20% paid channels, ~10% events.
- **$2M-10M ARR:** $15,000-50,000/mo. ~50% headcount, ~25% tools, ~15% paid, ~10% events and partnerships.

### CAC Benchmarks by ACV
- ACV under $5K: CAC under $1,000 (ideally under $500)
- ACV $5K-25K: CAC under $5,000
- ACV $25K-100K: CAC under $15,000
- ACV over $100K: CAC up to $30,000 acceptable if LTV supports

### Metrics That Matter
**Weekly (leading indicators, predict next quarter):** emails sent, open rate, reply rate, positive reply rate, LinkedIn connection rate, meetings booked, show rate, bounce rate.

**Monthly (lagging indicators, predict revenue):** CAC, LTV:CAC ratio (target >3:1), payback period (target <12 months), pipeline velocity, win rate, sales cycle length, NRR (target >110%).

**Pipeline velocity formula:** (qualified opps x average deal size x win rate) / average sales cycle in days. If velocity is declining QoQ, find which variable is deteriorating.

## Part 3 — Common GTM Anti-Patterns
- **Buying tools before proving the motion.** Tools do not fix bad strategy. Prove the motion manually first, automate what works.
- **Skipping ICP definition.** "Everyone who might buy" means irrelevant messaging. Build the ICP matrix before writing a single email. See ideal-customer-profile.
- **Hiring SDRs before founder-led sales works.** If the founder cannot sell it, an SDR cannot either. Founder closes the first 10-20 deals, then documents the playbook, then hires.
- **Measuring activity instead of outcomes.** 10,000 emails sent means nothing if zero convert. Track metrics tied to revenue, not vanity numbers.
- **Outbound without deliverability setup.** Warm up domains 2-3 weeks. Max 30-50 emails per inbox per day. Monitor sender reputation weekly. Set up SPF, DKIM, DMARC.
- **Scaling volume before fixing conversion.** 5,000 emails at 0.5% reply rate does not become 10,000 at 1%. It becomes 10,000 at 0.3% because you burned through your best prospects first. Get above 3% reply on a small list before scaling.
- **Copying a competitor's stack.** Their ICP, ACV, and stage differ from yours. Use as inspiration, not blueprint.
- **Same sequence for every prospect.** A VP Engineering and a VP Marketing have different pain points. Build persona-specific sequences.

## 30-Minute GTM Audit Checklist
Run through this for any B2B company. Score Yes/No per item. Any No is a gap.

**Data layer:** CRM is source of truth, bounce rate under 5%, dedup monthly, ICP documented.
**Enrichment layer:** At least one signal source active, accounts tiered, signals acted on within 7 days.
**Sequencing layer:** Multi-channel (not just email), persona-specific, reply rate above 2%, SPF/DKIM/DMARC set, under 50 emails/inbox/day.
**Pipeline layer:** Stages have entry/exit criteria, every deal has next step and close date, activities auto-logged, forecasts within 20% of actuals.
**Analytics layer:** Best channel by revenue (not activity), CAC and LTV calculated, weekly metrics review happens, win/loss reasons recorded.

- **16-18 Yes:** Strong foundation, focus on optimization.
- **10-15 Yes:** Gaps exist, prioritize the lowest-scoring layer.
- **Under 10 Yes:** Foundational rebuild needed, start with Layer 1 (data).

## What to Fix First
When the audit reveals multiple gaps, fix in this order:
1. Deliverability and data quality (if emails bounce or land in spam, nothing else matters)
2. ICP and targeting (wrong people, no amount of better copy helps)
3. Messaging and sequences (once reaching the right people reliably, optimize what you say)
4. Pipeline management (once meetings happen, make sure deals do not die)
5. Analytics and attribution (once the engine runs, measure and optimize)

Do not skip ahead. A company spending $5K/mo on intent data while emails land in spam is solving the wrong problem.

## Tips
- The best GTM strategy is the simplest one that works. One ICP, one channel, one message. Get that working, then expand.
- Start with your most confident customer segment, not the broadest one.
- Establish baseline metrics before launch or you cannot measure impact.
- Plan for feedback loops. Every assumption should have a date when you will test it.
- Focus on a few channels excellently rather than many channels poorly.
