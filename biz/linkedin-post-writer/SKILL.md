# LinkedIn Post Writer

## Overview
Write LinkedIn posts in your voice using 7 post frameworks (Hot Take, Framework, Story, Tool Review, Case Study, Giveaway, Milestone), 12 hook formulas, 5 CTA patterns matched to goal, formatting rules for mobile readability, and a scoring rubric to ensure every draft meets a quality bar. Use this item after you have a voice profile from at least 10 past posts. Without voice examples, output will sound generic.

## When to Use
- Drafting LinkedIn posts on any topic (frameworks, hot takes, milestones, tool reviews)
- Turning raw ideas, screenshots, or outlines into polished posts
- Repurposing long-form content (blog, podcast, case study) into LinkedIn format
- Reactive content for industry news, tool launches, or trending topics
- Building a weekly content calendar with varied post types
- Quality-checking drafts before publishing

## Before You Write
Ask the user for, or confirm you have:
1. Core idea (a specific insight, result, experience, or opinion — not "write about marketing")
2. Audience (job titles, seniority, industry)
3. Goal (pick one: brand awareness, engagement, lead generation, thought leadership, hiring)
4. Past posts to calibrate voice (10+ examples ideal, 3-5 helpful, none = generic output)
5. Data, stories, or proof points to include
6. Preferred post type (or "you pick")

Do not draft until you have answers to at least questions 1, 2, and 4. Without voice examples, the output will be mediocre.

## Voice Calibration
Generic LinkedIn content is obvious and gets ignored. Voice calibration is what separates a post that sounds like you from one that sounds like an AI wrote it.

### Voice Extraction Process
When given 10+ past posts, extract and document all of the following before writing anything.

**Step 1: Sentence analysis.** Average sentence length (count words in 20 random sentences). Fragment usage ("Built this. Shipped it. Worked."). Frequency of "I" openers vs other openers.

**Step 2: Opening patterns.** How does the author typically open? (Bold claim, question, story moment, stat.) List the 5 most common first-line structures.

**Step 3: Vocabulary fingerprint.** Build a language swap table: words the author actually uses vs words they never use. Note signature phrases and repeated expressions. Flag jargon patterns (do they name-drop tools, use acronyms, spell things out?).

| I Say This | Not This |
|---|---|
| [extracted from posts] | [common alternatives they avoid] |
| [extracted from posts] | [common alternatives they avoid] |

**Step 4: Structural habits.** Do they use numbered lists, bullet points, or neither? How do they transition between sections (line breaks, transition words)? What is their typical post structure (hook → context → list → CTA)?

**Step 5: Tone markers.** Where do they fall on these spectrums?

- [Arrogant] — [Confident] — [Humble]
- [Academic] — [Practical] — [Simplistic]
- [Safe] — [Slightly edgy] — [Inflammatory]
- [Polished] — [Conversational] — [Unfiltered]

**Step 6: Emoji and formatting rules.** How often do they use emojis? Which specific emojis? Do they use bold text and how?

**Step 7: CTA patterns.** How do they end posts? (Question, statement, comment trigger, nothing.) Do they ever use "follow for more" style CTAs?

### Voice Quick-Check
Before presenting any draft, verify:
- Could this post have been written by anyone? If yes, it fails.
- Read the first 3 sentences aloud. Do they sound like the author's past posts?
- Check the language swap table. Are any "Not This" words in the draft?
- Is the emoji frequency correct?
- Does the ending match the author's typical pattern?

## The 7 Post Frameworks
Pick one framework per post.

### 1. Hot Take
Contrarian opinion that challenges conventional wisdom. Designed to provoke engagement through disagreement and "yes, finally someone said it" reactions.

**Structure:** Bold contrarian statement (1 sentence, max 15 words). 1-2 sentences of context (why most people are wrong). 3-5 sentences building your argument with specifics, not generalities. 1-line closer that reinforces the take, no hedging. Polarizing CTA question that forces a stance.

Example hook: "Your CRM is not a strategy. It is a spreadsheet with a login page."

When to use: industry debates, challenging popular tools or beliefs, reframing something everyone takes for granted. Works best when you have a strong opinion backed by experience.

Length: 500-800 characters. Hot takes lose power when they ramble.

### 2. Framework Post
Step-by-step system or process. The core authority-builder. Positions you as someone who has done the work, not just thought about it.

**Structure:** Hook (result or bold claim about what the framework produces). 1-2 lines of context (who this is for, why it matters). Numbered list of 5-9 steps with clear title and 1-2 lines of detail each. 1-line closer (the outcome of following the framework). CTA: "Comment [KEYWORD] for the full playbook" or a question.

Example hook: "Every outbound campaign we run follows the same 7 steps. Here is the framework."

When to use: sharing processes, systems, playbooks, or mental models you actually use. Include real tool names, real numbers, real steps.

Length: 1,000-1,500 characters. Framework posts earn their length through density.

### 3. Story Post
Personal narrative that teaches through experience. Highest emotional engagement of any format.

**Structure:** Hook (the most interesting moment, not the beginning — drop reader into the middle of action). Set the scene (2-3 sentences: where, when, stakes). Conflict or turning point (3-5 sentences: what went wrong, what changed, what you did not expect). Lesson or outcome (2-3 sentences: what you learned that the reader can apply). CTA: question inviting shared experience.

Example hook: "I mass-applied for 300 jobs. Not a single reply. Then I sent one DM."

When to use: founder journey moments, career pivots, failures, moments where you learned something the hard way. The story must serve the lesson.

Length: 800-1,200 characters.

### 4. Tool Review / Stack Breakdown
Authoritative review of tools you have actually used. Credibility comes from volume tested and honest verdicts.

**Structure:** Hook (number of tools tested + bold verdict or ranking). Brief context (why you tested, what you were solving for). List of each tool with 1-2 line verdict ("Found 40% more emails than Tool B" not "Good UI"). Summary recommendation with caveats by use case. CTA: "Save this for your next stack review" or "What did I miss?".

Example hook: "I tested 12 email finding tools over 90 days. Here is the honest ranking."

When to use: software reviews, tech stack reveals, tool comparisons. Only write these if you have actually used the tools.

Length: 800-1,500 characters.

### 5. Case Study / Results Post
Sharing real results with enough detail to be credible. The proof post that converts followers into leads.

**Structure:** Hook (headline metric, one number, one result). Context (who, what, timeframe — "A B2B SaaS company with 50 employees" is fine). The approach (3-5 bullets: tool names, sequence details, targeting criteria). Results (specific numbers, before/after — "47 qualified meetings in 60 days" not "Increased pipeline"). Takeaway (the one lesson that generalizes). CTA: question about their experience with similar problems.

Example hook: "47 qualified meetings in 60 days. Zero cold calls. Here is the system."

When to use: client wins (with permission), campaign results, business milestones. Every claim needs a number.

Length: 800-1,200 characters.

### 6. Giveaway / Lead Magnet
Offering something free in exchange for engagement. High reach, moderate quality leads. The comment-trigger mechanic is the engine.

**Structure:** Hook (what you are giving away + why it is valuable, lead with the outcome not the format — "The system that booked 200 meetings" beats "A PDF checklist"). 2-3 lines on what is inside, be specific ("12 cold email templates with subject lines and reply rates" not "A helpful resource about email"). Social proof (who else has used it, what results). CTA: "Comment [KEYWORD] and I will send it over".

Example hook: "I am giving away our entire outbound playbook. The same one we use for clients paying $10k/month."

When to use: playbooks, templates, checklists, recordings, swipe files. The giveaway must be genuinely useful.

Length: 500-800 characters. The value is in the asset, not the post.

### 7. Milestone / Journey Post
Sharing a business or personal milestone with enough vulnerability and specifics to be relatable, not just bragging.

**Structure:** Hook (the milestone as a contrast or transformation — before/after is the strongest opener). Context (where you started, be honest, the worse the "before" the better the story). 3-5 bullets on what actually happened (key decisions, turning points, things that did not work). What you would do differently or what surprised you. CTA: question inviting others to share their own milestone or journey.

Example hook: "No clients. No revenue. No team. That was 8 months ago. Here is what happened."

When to use: revenue milestones, team growth, product launches, follower milestones. Only works if you share real details.

Length: 800-1,200 characters.

## The 12 Hook Formulas
The hook is 80% of the battle. If nobody stops scrolling, the rest does not matter.

| # | Formula | Example | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Bold claim + number | "We booked 320 meetings in October." | Case studies, results posts |
| 2 | Contrarian statement | "Your CRM is lying to you." | Hot takes, industry commentary |
| 3 | Story intro (start in middle) | "I mass-applied for 300 jobs. Not a single reply." | Origin stories, pivots |
| 4 | News hook | "Google just released their AI agent platform. Here is what changes." | Reactive, tool launches |
| 5 | System reveal | "I am giving away our entire outbound system. For free." | Giveaways, lead magnets |
| 6 | Stat + implication | "11.8k emails sent. 10.2% reply rate. 178 opportunities." | Case studies, breakdowns |
| 7 | Before/after | "No revenue. No pipeline. No team. That was 7 months ago." | Milestones, transformations |
| 8 | Direct question | "How many tools in your stack are actually contributing to pipeline?" | Thought leadership |
| 9 | Admission of failure | "This campaign flopped. 2,000 emails. 3 replies. Here is what went wrong." | Story posts, lessons |
| 10 | Pattern interrupt | "Stop building funnels." | Hot takes, contrarian |
| 11 | Curiosity gap | "The highest-converting email I ever sent was 19 words." | Framework posts, case studies |
| 12 | Social proof stack | "200+ companies. 12 months. One playbook." | Giveaways, credibility |

**Hook rules:**
- The first line must stand alone. It should stop someone mid-scroll even without the rest of the post.
- No emojis in hooks. Ever.
- No questions answerable with "no" and a scroll. ("Do you want to improve your marketing?" — scroll.)
- Specific beats vague. "320 meetings" beats "hundreds of meetings." "19 words" beats "a short email."
- The hook is a promise. The post must deliver on it.
- Write 3 hooks before picking one. Your first hook is almost never your best.

## Formatting Rules
LinkedIn's feed is narrow, especially on mobile. Dense paragraphs die.

### Line Spacing
- **Line break between every 1-2 sentences.** Single most important rule. No paragraph should be more than 2 sentences.
- Double line break (blank line) between sections: hook, body, list, closer, CTA.
- After writing, mentally preview on a phone screen. If any section looks like a wall, break it up.

### Numbered Lists
- Use number emojis or plain numbers for lists of 3+ items.
- Each item: number + title on one line, then 1-2 short bullet points underneath.
- Keep bullets to one line each. No sub-bullets, no nested lists.
- If a list has 7+ items, consider splitting into a carousel instead of a text post.

### Length Targets
| Post Type | Character Target | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Hot take | 500-800 | Short and sharp. Loses power if it rambles. |
| Framework | 1,000-1,500 | Earns length through density. |
| Story | 800-1,200 | Long enough for tension, short enough for attention. |
| Tool review | 800-1,500 | Dense information. Every line teaches. |
| Case study | 800-1,200 | Tight and metric-heavy. |
| Giveaway | 500-800 | Value is in the asset, not the post. |
| Milestone | 800-1,200 | Specifics, not filler. |

LinkedIn shows ~210 characters before the "see more" fold on mobile. Your hook + first line of context must fit in that window. If the reader does not click "see more," the rest does not exist.

### General Formatting Rules
- Use dashes (-) for bullet points. Arrows for emphasis or flow.
- Bold sparingly. Use bold for section headers in long posts or to highlight one key phrase per section.
- No hashtags in the body. If you must use them, put 3-5 at the very end, separated by a line break.
- Never tag someone just for reach. Only tag if they are directly referenced or will genuinely engage.
- No links in the post body. LinkedIn's algorithm suppresses posts with external links. If you must link, put it in the first comment.

## The 5 CTA Patterns
Every post should end with a reason for the reader to do something. Match the CTA to your goal.

**1. Comment trigger (Goal: reach + lead capture).** "Comment [KEYWORD] and I will send you the full system." Best for giveaways, frameworks, playbooks. Best for lead gen. Pair with an automation that actually delivers.

**2. Save prompt (Goal: bookmarks + long-term visibility).** "Save this for your next stack review." Bookmarks count as engagement and keep the post circulating.

**3. Polarizing question (Goal: comments + debate).** "Is cold email dead or are most people just bad at it?" Must have two clear sides. "What do you think about marketing?" generates nothing.

**4. Shared experience (Goal: community + relatability).** "Anyone else had a campaign that looked perfect on paper and completely bombed?" Invites people to share their own version.

**5. Follow prompt (Goal: follower growth).** "Follow for weekly breakdowns of what is actually working in B2B outbound." Only use if the post itself proves you are worth following.

## Scoring Rubric
Score every draft before presenting. Do not present anything below 4.0 out of 5.

| Criterion | Weight | 5 (Ship it) | 3 (Needs work) | 1 (Rewrite) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hook strength | 25% | Stops the scroll, specific number or bold claim, earns the "see more" click | Decent but vague, could be sharper | Generic, boring, starts with "I'm excited," easy to skip |
| Voice match | 25% | Sounds exactly like the author, passes the language swap table, no AI giveaway words | Close but has some off-brand phrasing | Could be anyone, no personality, reads like ChatGPT default |
| Substance | 20% | Teaches something specific and actionable, reader can do something differently | Has value but stays surface-level, "good advice" without specifics | Platitudes and generalities |
| Structure | 15% | Clean formatting, right length for the post type, line breaks in the right places | Minor formatting issues, a section too dense | Wall of text, no visual hierarchy, scrolled past on a phone |
| CTA quality | 15% | Natural CTA matched to post type and goal, easy to act on, one CTA not three | Decent but generic, "What do you think?" without specificity | Missing entirely, or forced/desperate ("LIKE SHARE FOLLOW") |

**Scoring example:** Hook 5/5 (specific number, stops the scroll). Voice 4/5 (strong match, but "utilize" in paragraph 3 is off-brand). Substance 5/5 (actionable framework with real tool names). Structure 4/5 (slightly over length target). CTA 5/5 (clean comment trigger). Overall 4.6/5 — ready to ship after swapping "utilize" for "use."

## Weekly Content Mix (3 posts per week)
- Monday or Tuesday: Framework or Case Study (authority builder, start the week with substance)
- Wednesday or Thursday: Hot Take or Tool Review (engagement driver, mid-week debate and saves)
- Friday: Story, Milestone, or Giveaway (community builder, end the week on a personal or generous note)

Never post the same framework two weeks in a row. Alternate between "I" posts (personal stories, milestones) and "you" posts (frameworks, tools, how-tos). One giveaway per month maximum. Reactive content (news hooks) can replace any scheduled post when the timing is right.

### Batch Writing Workflow
1. Pick 3 topics for the week.
2. Write 3 hooks for each topic (9 total).
3. Pick the best hook for each topic.
4. Draft all 3 posts in one session.
5. Score each draft. Rewrite anything below 4.0.
6. Schedule or post.

## Anti-Patterns
- "I'm so humbled and grateful..." — performative. Start with what happened, not how you feel about it.
- "Excited to announce..." — overused. Lead with the thing itself: "We just shipped X" or "X is live."
- "Here are 5 tips to improve your..." — generic. Replace "tips" with a specific framework.
- "Let that sink in." — condescending. Cut it.
- Starting with "So..." — filler. Delete it. Start with the next word.
- "I failed and that is okay" — self-help tone. Share the failure but lead with the lesson or the system you built to prevent it.
- Ending with a question nobody would answer — too broad. Ask something specific.
- Walls of text with no line breaks — instant scroll-past. Break every 1-2 sentences.
- Humble-bragging disguised as a lesson — annoying. Own the win directly, or make the post about the system, not the number.
- Achievement post with no takeaway — nobody cares about your win unless they can learn something. Every win post needs at least 3 specific bullets.
- Opening with "I" in 3+ consecutive posts — signals self-absorption. Alternate openers.
- Overusing "game-changer" or "next level" — marketing cliches. Be specific about what changed and by how much.
- Posting a screenshot with no context — meaningless without the story. Always add 3+ lines explaining what the reader is looking at and why it matters.
- "Agree? Thoughts?" — lazy CTA. No one feels compelled to respond to a vague prompt.
- "Like and share if you found this helpful!" — begging for engagement. Signals low confidence.
- "Link in comments" without adding one — broken promise. Trains your audience to distrust you.
- "Drop a comment below!" — generic. No reason or topic.
- Double CTA — pick one.

## Tips
- Start from something specific. A data point, a screenshot, a customer conversation, a tool you just tested. Never start from "write a LinkedIn post about X."
- One idea per post. Posts that try to cover five topics perform worse than posts that go deep on one.
- Read it aloud. If any sentence sounds like marketing copy, rewrite until it sounds like something you would say to a peer over coffee.
- Track what works. Keep a running log with engagement data (impressions, likes, comments, reposts). After 20+ posts, patterns emerge. Double down.
- Repurpose aggressively. A single long-form piece (podcast, blog, presentation) can produce 5-10 posts by pulling out individual insights.
- Engagement in the first 60 minutes drives reach. Reply to every comment quickly. Ask follow-up questions. The algorithm rewards conversations, not just reactions.
- Post timing matters less than consistency. Tuesday-Thursday 8-10am is conventional wisdom, but a great post at 3pm will outperform a mediocre post at 9am.
- Never post and ghost. If you are not around to reply to comments for at least an hour, delay the post.
